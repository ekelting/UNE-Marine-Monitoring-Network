⛵ UNE trip check

For boat captains and PIs planning a trip from the UNE boat launch. It rates each distance band from today's readings. The boat captain makes the final call.

Built from the following (in order)

  1. NOAA alerts. Any Small Craft Advisory (or anything stronger) for our zone, Cape Elizabeth to the Merrimack River (ANZ154), means no trips in open water. That's UNE policy. The Saco River estuary, from the mouth up to the Biddeford dams, is still OK.
  2. Waves at the local buoy (Camp Ellis). Over about 2–3 ft is usually too rough, but the lab's PI sets the limit.
  3. Wave direction. Waves from the E, ESE or SE have no protection. From the N or NE it can be OK if you can find shelter behind land.
  4. Wind direction. Wind from the west is blocked by the coast near shore. Wind from the east has nothing to stop it.
  5. Plan before leaving the dock. Trips go as far as 40 nm north, but never more than 25 nm from land.

The boat captain makes the final call. This page only sorts each distance band into "looks OK", "captain's call" or "stay ashore" to start that conversation.

Draft numbers to confirm

These are placeholders, not the captain's rules: captain's call from 15 kt of wind (12 kt from the east), stay ashore from 20 kt (25 kt for a west wind near shore); waves within half a foot of the PI's limit count as captain's call; the local buoy is used inside 3 nm and NOAA buoy 44007 beyond; an onshore wind on a falling tide is captain's call at the river mouth; a Dense Fog or Freezing Spray Advisory is captain's call; for the estuary, captain's call from 20 kt and stay ashore from 25 kt. River current after heavy rain isn't included yet.

Data: UNE Camp Ellis wave buoy and Ram Island weather station; NOAA NDBC buoy 44007; NOAA tide predictions for Camp Ellis (8418606); National Weather Service alerts. Not for navigation.

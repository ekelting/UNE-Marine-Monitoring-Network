🎵 Can radar hear the wave rhythm? 🧪 experiment

When the sea is rough, each patch of water flickers on radar in time with the waves passing through. We time that flicker and compare it with the buoy's wave period. Dots on the dashed line mean a perfect match. Only confident readings are shown.

🧠 How does a wave radar work?

📡

1. Spin and ping

The antenna spins about once every 1.3 seconds, firing short microwave pulses. "X-band" means the microwaves are about 3 cm long, roughly the size of a wind ripple.

〰️

2. Ripples bounce it back

Tiny wind ripples act like millions of little mirrors that send the pulse back to the radar. Bigger waves tilt and hide those ripples, which makes bright and dark stripes. A glassy calm sea reflects the beam away like a mirror, so it looks black.

🎞️

3. Make a movie

Each hourly scan stacks 229 sweeps into a 5-minute movie. Things that stay put (land, rocks, moorings) are land; things that move are water and boats.

🧮

4. Turn pictures into numbers

This page measures every scan: how much the sea sparkles, how much it moves, where water is breaking, and what's moving fast. Then it checks those numbers against the wave buoy next door.

📖 Glossary: what do these words mean?
Scan
One 5-minute burst of radar sweeps, recorded about once an hour. CUMULUS calls it a "collect".
Sweep
One full turn of the antenna: a single radar picture. A scan has about 229 of them.
✨ Sea sparkle
The share of open water that lit up on an average sweep, measured in a ring 300–1,500 m from the radar. More wind → more ripples → more sparkle. It's a relative index, not a wave height.
Rougher than X%
Where today's sparkle ranks among every scan this radar has taken. 90% means rougher-looking than 9 out of 10 scans.
🎨 Motion
How much each spot's echo changed during the 5 minutes. Open water flickers constantly; land barely changes.
💥 Whitewater
Water that's bright and moving within 150 m of the shore: mostly breaking surf. (Boat wakes near shore can count too.)
🚤 Moving target / track
Something the radar followed from sweep to sweep, usually a boat. The tracker is automatic, so it's "probably a boat", not a guaranteed one.
Knot (kn)
Nautical speed: 1 knot ≈ 1.15 mph ≈ 0.51 m/s.
Wave height
From the Camp Ellis buoy: the average height of the biggest third of the waves, from trough to crest.
Wave period / rhythm
Seconds between wave crests. Short = local wind chop. Long (10 s+) = swell from far away.
r (correlation)
How tightly two things rise and fall together: +1 = in perfect step, 0 = unrelated, −1 = opposites.
